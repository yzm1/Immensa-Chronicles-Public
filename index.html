Immensa Chronicles

The world keeps going.

A one-way road can carry a dying child nine hundred miles before nightfall—and leave a family twenty-three years apart. Dragons write the names of the dead across miles of evening sky. A stair descends past its thousandth landing to a black sea, a harbor, and more stairs below.

Immensa is an ongoing fantasy worldbuilding and fiction project by Yali Zak-Malkin: a world so vast the horizon seems flat, so old that the ancients had ancients, and so long shaped by Classes, Skills and advancement that progression itself has an archaeology.

Three glimpses

Before the explanation, the world.

Immensa is not meant to be understood from a list of systems. It is meant to be encountered: through roads with impossible terms, places that keep opening outward, old powers with their own ordinary lives, and people who still have to decide what to do next.

The road

Nine hundred miles. One day. One direction.

Onceways make impossible distance traversable without making it cheap. People cross for medicine, trade, escape, ambition and love. The way around may be nine thousand miles. The choice remains human even when the road is not.

The world can be immense and intimate in the same scene.
The sky

Some names need a mile of sky to say.

Above the Long Vanes, dragons are not encounters waiting to happen. They have borders, law, grief, memory and names written in flight. A dragon legendary to humans may still be considered a promising youth by older wings.

A dragon can simply be a dragon—and still have a civilization.
The descent

The thousandth landing is not the bottom.

The Red Chasm Stair passes inns, shrines, a forest growing sideways, an abandoned village and weather inside the earth. At the landing surface explorers made into myth, there is a black sea, ships, city lights—and the Stair continues down.

Every sufficiently large answer should reveal another horizon.

What kind of world is this?

Everything important arrives with a past.

Classes go extinct. Skills survive as folk traditions. Roads outlive the places they once reached. Professions inherit titles nobody qualifies for anymore. Creatures keep migrating toward habitats that vanished ages ago. Ancient scholars excavated older ruins, argued about them, and sometimes got them wrong.

People enter all of this mid-conversation. They inherit laws, tools, grudges, recipes, gods, neighborhoods, monsters, jokes, debts and roads already worn by other feet.

Advancement does not carry you away to a higher world. It makes the world you already live in larger.

Immensa is deliberately recognizable fantasy: guilds, parties, dragons, monster hunts, magical materials, Classes, breakthroughs, dungeons, legendary craftspeople and ridiculous abilities are welcome. The difference is that none of them arrived yesterday. Tens of thousands of years of people have lived with them, built around them, misunderstood them, improved them, broken them and left things behind.

Wonder before paperwork.

A forest with trees tens of miles high is allowed to be beautiful or terrifying before anyone explains who maintains the road through it. The consequences matter because the wonder matters first.

Ordinary life survives the extraordinary.

There are inns, bakeries, children, bad coffee, jokes, pets, repairs, festivals and people going home. A world wonder can have weeds. A frightening creature can play. A civilization-changing resource can make excellent soup.

No single golden age.

One age mastered routes, another memory, another living bodies, another something modern people no longer know how to name. Modern societies are genuinely better at other things. No civilization owns the past.

The map is allowed to lose.

A frontier can turn out to be somebody else’s interior. A dungeon can contain a civilization. A mountain can later be understood as part of something larger. The revelation widens the same world instead of replacing it.

About

A worldbuilding project that is becoming a story.

Immensa Chronicles is an ongoing worldbuilding and fiction project by Yali Zak-Malkin. Hundreds of short stories, essays and interlocking world records are being used to discover the setting from different lives, scales and eras rather than define it all from above.

Some pieces become foundations. Some are revised or contradicted. Places recur and gain history. Large areas remain untouched. The aim is not a perfectly complete encyclopedia; it is a world with enough depth, texture, danger, beauty, humor and unfinished horizon to keep producing stories.

Where it is going

Immensa is being developed toward a long-form progression-fantasy / LitRPG serial. The current fiction and worldbuilding are ways of finding what deserves to survive into that story—and making sure that when the protagonist begins in one small place, there is genuinely more world beyond every horizon.

Development process & AI use

AI tools are used extensively in research, corpus analysis, prototyping, drafting and software development. Creative direction, selection, revision, curation and final decisions about the project are by Yali Zak-Malkin. AI is part of how Immensa is being built, not what Immensa is about.
